### Step-1. The plan text of 20.1 still describes a scenario that cannot happen

Quote: Plan: "sets a policy `max-length=0` with `overflow=reject-publish` on the dead-letter queue ... a handler always fails; asserts the message is still in the source queue". Test: two messages, `dead_letter_worker_publisher_confirm_timeout 5000` on its own container, a 7 s window with 1 message in the source queue and 1 in the dead-letter queue, then the policy is deleted and the dead-letter queue holds 2. Plan r6 was not changed. `spec.md` 20.1 and `qa.md` do not decide the policy or the timeout.
Consequence: with one message the dead-letter queue would take it, because quorum reject-publish accepts the first one. The plan step cannot be run as written, and the next reader who follows it gets a test that fails.
Belongs to: plan
Resolution: fixed in plan r7 by the coordinator, as above.

### Step-2. An interrupt during the retry back-off leaves the message unacked

Quote: `pause`: `catch (InterruptedException e) { Thread.currentThread().interrupt(); throw error; }`. No test runs this branch. Neither the step nor the spec says what happens to a message when the consumer stops during back-off.
Consequence: the message is rethrown as a failed handling, so the container can requeue it or dead-letter it, depending on the exception type and the container stop. The behaviour is not decided, and no test fixes it. Kafka's consumer has the same open point.
Belongs to: plan
Resolution: accepted by the coordinator under Q30: an interrupt during the back-off rethrows, so the message goes back to the queue and is delivered again; at-least-once delivery and idempotent handlers make that safe.
